# WINS

> **Rough draft.** Seeded by an agent from public writing in [btopro/blog](https://github.com/btopro/blog), [btopro.wordpress.com](https://btopro.wordpress.com), [btopro/edtechjoker](https://github.com/btopro/edtechjoker) and three recent talks: *The Sustainable Impact and Innovation Pipeline (SIIP)*, *The Many Mes* and *So you want a (web) revolution?*. Every entry names its source. Items marked **verify** need a number or detail confirmed before anyone quotes them. Visuals live in [`visuals/`](visuals/README.md).

Stories fade faster than code. This file keeps proof that the worldview works, so agents, contributors and future maintainers can point to it. A good win has a **person**, a **before/after**, and a **source**.

## How to add a win

```
### Short title (year)
- Who: the person or group it helped
- Before → after: what changed, with a number if there is one
- Why it matters: which WORLDVIEW pillar it proves
- For partners / institutions: the outcome in their terms (optional)
- Source: link
```

Only list wins that have happened. Proposals and pilots in progress stay out until they land.

## People empowered

### Learn to Flourish Hub: flourish.hhd.psu.edu (2026)
- Who: the Learn to Flourish Hub, led by Director **Dr. Blake Colaianne** (Assistant Professor, Human Development and Family Studies), with its faculty and students, working with the Office of Digital Learning in Arts and Architecture.
- Before → after: Hub faculty built a polished, professional public site on HAX, the face of the Hub for donors, students and partners, served from hax.psu.edu. It launched in September 2026 to an enthusiastic response from faculty and donors.
- Why it matters: a partner outside the College of Arts and Architecture owns and runs its own web presence without a web team (pillars 1, 2 and 4). It also shows HAX works beyond courses.
- For partners / institutions: a cross-college collaboration where HAX was the enabler and the Hub is the story.
- Source: [flourish.hhd.psu.edu](https://flourish.hhd.psu.edu), [team](https://flourish.hhd.psu.edu/team)

### The seven accent-cards
- Who: an instructional design support specialist building a photography course.
- Before → after: a dated, uneditable image explaining color would have needed an image editor and specialist skills. He placed seven `accent-card`s, wrote each wavelength on one, and built the explanation himself, with color contrast handled by the component.
- Why it matters: non-technical authors produce accessible, creative work (pillars 5 and 2).
- Source: [Open Community Pedagogy](https://btopro.com/work/open-community-pedagogy)

### From HAX Lab to industry
- Who: former HAX Lab students and HAX contributors.
- Before → after: they now work at **Red Hat, Adobe, IBM, Ford and PennDOT**, using web component skills in their daily work. ### Site Dashboard, three generations of students (2022–2026)
- Who: student teams across four years.

Ashley came in with almost no coding experience and rated herself "drowning" on day one; she left confident enough to turn her designs into code. It changed who Bryan looks for: "empathetic design people that can think and code."

### SIIP lessons learned
- The biggest gains come **6–12 months after** students take the class.
- The **top 5–10%** of a class often choose to go further.
- The class works best as 40% skilling up, 20% a simple non-community project, 20% a larger, less-scaffolded project, and 20% an unscaffolded, internship-like project.
- Open office hours three times a week now draw contributors who were never in HAX Lab.
- HAX is the backdrop, not the subject: IST 256 teaches modern front-end development and happens to use HAX and DDD. A gap in the docs becomes a contribution, so fixing community docs is part of onboarding.
- Students get visible credit: Bryan posts their work on LinkedIn and tags them.
- Source: [SIIP talk, Apereo microconference, May 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNF0uzYi9Kk). The pedagogy is cognitive apprenticeship (modeling, scaffolding, fading, coaching), framed by Bill Rose.

### Agents with context: 90% then 211%
- Who: the HAX project, with agents primed by PRAW.
- Before → after: versions 1–25 averaged about 33 closed issues per month. Version 25→26 reached about 64 per month (+90%). Version 26→26.7 reached about 104 per month (+211%). Using AI without context first cost about 3% efficiency. By late 2026 the project is merging 400+ pull requests a month.
- Why it matters: the worldview, rules and skills in this repository are measurably what made agents useful.
- Source: *The Many Mes* talk; Bryan Ollendyke (Oct 2026)

### Twelve of me
- Who: Bryan, with agents primed by PRAW.
- Before → after: twelve agent copies working from `WORLDVIEW.md` and the indexed codebase did more in 24 hours than he could previously finish in six months.
- Why it matters: the worldview scales beyond one person, which is the point of this repository.
- Source: [The many me's](https://btopro.com/hax/the-many-me-s)

### Every site since 2020 still works
- Who: everyone who has ever published with HAX.
- Before → after: because HAX saves HTML and ships the tag definitions separately, every site made since about 2020 still works; it just looks better and is easier to edit, because the definitions were upgraded underneath it. The 2018 save button and today's are the same asset.
- Why it matters: content outlives the tool's redesigns (Sustainable, Platform agnostic).
- Source: [Nothing is intentional, until it is, Mar 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wkdW2EjkwZ4)

### Reach by early 2026
- Who: students and authors at Penn State and beyond.
- Before → after: about 67,000 students educated through HAX-based systems in the College of Arts and Architecture alone (the real number is far higher); themes grew from 14 at the end of 2024 to more than 30; over 10,000 pages of unique content on hax.psu.edu by early 2025; external adopters such as the LaunchBox small-business training site, which built on hax.psu.edu and then downloaded its site to its own server.
- Why it matters: scale without a vendor (Free and open, Platform agnostic).
- Source: [Nothing is intentional, until it is, Mar 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wkdW2EjkwZ4), [2025: State of HAX Community, Feb 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WGv94SY0cRM)

### Migrations in seconds, not months
- Who: instructional design teams and faculty.
- Before → after: ELMS:LN courses that took months or years to build with faculty were migrated to HAX by pointing at their URL. Word files with good heading structure become sites; Notion, GitBook, Drupal book (5 and later), Pressbooks and existing HAX sites can be imported. Bryan: a two-page nested site took about 20 minutes in WordPress and a 20-page outline about two minutes in HAX.
- Why it matters: meet people where they are (Remixable, Platform agnostic).
- Source: [HAX Horizon Report, Oct 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bX04I1Cw9FA), [HiTech Podcast ep. 168, Oct 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fKeSEZm3khE)

## Cost and access

### Fast without a build step
- Who: anyone publishing a HAX site.
- Before → after: authors click through a CMS-style UI and get an offline-capable PWA with high performance scores, while shipping unbundled ES modules.
- Why it matters: quality by default for non-developers (pillars 1 and 5).
- Source: [THE web components high performance video](https://btopro.com/work/the-web-components-high-performance-video)

### Skeletons and benevolent restrictions (HAX 25)
- Who: new site builders.
- Before → after: from blank templates to full working starter sites ("skeletons") anyone can export and hand to someone else, hundreds of clicks closer to done. "Benevolent restrictions" is Bill Rose's phrase.
- Why it matters: constraints that help people start (pillars 2 and 5).
- Source: [Benevolent restrictions](https://btopro.com/hax/benevolent-restrictions)

## Foundations (before HAX)

These come from the Drupal and ELMS:LN years. They are foundational to Bryan and to how HAX came to be, but they are legacy, not what HAX is today. Use them for origin stories; don't lead with them or present ELMS:LN as current technology.

### Where it started: the outline designer
- Who: authors of Drupal sites worldwide.
- Before → after: early in his Penn State career Bryan built the outline designer to make course structure fast to edit; as open source it has "empowered tens of thousands of people over the years that we'll never meet to build websites more quickly." Its successor in HAX is still mission-critical.
- Source: [Nothing is intentional, until it is, Mar 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wkdW2EjkwZ4)

### Drupal at Penn State, by giving it away
- Who: Penn State's web community.
- Before → after: Bryan moved a Dreamweaver course to Drupal over one weekend, gave Drupal-based systems away to form a community, and contributed more than 100 Drupal modules. Penn State went from unfriendly to open source to Drupal as a de facto standard; this became the *Information Altruism* thesis.
- Source: [HiTech Podcast ep. 168, Oct 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fKeSEZm3khE)

### A $250 textbook becomes a $30 fee
- Who: students in ARTH 112 at Penn State.
- Before → after: about $250 per student down to a $30 fee, delivered on ELMS:LN. This was the work that came before HAX and set up everything after it.
- Why it matters: open resources collapse the cost of knowledge (pillars 3 and 5).
- Source: *Universities can't make software* on [btopro.wordpress.com](https://btopro.wordpress.com)

### Hundreds of thousands of lines removed
- Who: ELMS:LN and everyone maintaining it.
- Before → after: web components let the platform delete hundreds of thousands of lines of code and more than 50 modules in a year.
- Why it matters: sustainable, maintainable systems (pillar 7).
- Source: [The Climb](https://btopro.com/work/the-climb)

### Live swap, thousands of students, about three tickets
- Who: students in production courses.
- Before → after: V1-spec web components replaced the old front end in live courses around March of that year, with only about three users reporting issues.
- Why it matters: change can be safe and invisible to learners (pillars 5 and 7).
- Source: [The Climb](https://btopro.com/work/the-climb)
