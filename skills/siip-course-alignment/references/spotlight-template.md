# SIIP spotlight template

A spotlight tells one person's story: a student, alum or faculty member whose work moved an open source project forward. Spotlights are the visible credit that keeps the pipeline turning, and the evidence that convinces the next faculty partner. They only go out with the person's permission.

## Permission first

Get this before drafting anything public. Keep a record of it (an email or message is enough).

- [ ] The person said yes in writing to being featured, and knows where it will appear (repo, site, talk, social post).
- [ ] They chose what to share: name or initials, photo, quote, current employer or school, links (GitHub, LinkedIn, portfolio).
- [ ] They reviewed the final text and any quote attributed to them, and approved it.
- [ ] They know they can ask for it to be changed or taken down at any time, and who to ask.
- [ ] Nothing comes from education records (grades, enrollment status, disciplinary or accommodation details) unless the student chose to share it. In the US that's FERPA territory; check your institution's rules.
- [ ] The person is an adult. Don't spotlight minors without a parent or guardian's written consent and your institution's approval.
- [ ] Faculty and staff: check whether their unit has its own approval step for public features.

Asking is part of the credit. Most people say yes; anyone who says no still gets thanked by name in the project's usual places (release notes, commit credit) if they want.

## Template

```
### Name: short headline (year or years)
- Who: name, role then (course, lab, club, faculty partner) → role now (optional)
- Before → after: what they built or changed, with a number if there is one
- Their words: one short quote they approved (optional)
- Why it matters: which part of the pipeline it shows (course, lab, club, mentoring, faculty funding)
- Links: their chosen links, plus the PRs, issues or release that hold the work
- Permission: how and when they agreed (e.g. "email, Oct 2026"); kept out of public copies if they prefer
```

## Good spotlights

- **Name the work, not just the person.** Link the pull requests, issues, themes or tools they shipped so the credit is checkable.
- **Show the arc.** Course → lab or club → bigger contribution → mentoring others is the pattern worth making visible.
- **Use their words.** One short, approved quote beats a paragraph of praise.
- **Credit faculty for what they enabled:** a problem they brought, a grant or project they funded, students they sent.
- **No money figures** unless the person asks; measure by what got built and who it helped.
- **Keep it current.** Revisit once a year; update roles with permission, and remove anything the person asks to remove.
