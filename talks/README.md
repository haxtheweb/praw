## HiTech Podcast ep. 168: Reclaiming the Internet (Oct 2024)

- **Hosts:** Josh Schwartz and Will Illingworth, both instructional designers; Will knew Bryan from Penn State Harrisburg
- **Origin story:** Keith Bailey writing pagination JavaScript as director; Bryan's weekend Drupal course; ELMS; giving systems away to form a community ("if you give away a box of kittens, people now need to maintain kittens"); the thesis written in first and third person; a 2015 napkin idea; Mike Potter pointing to the web component *standard* rather than a component library; three weeks off in 2017 to rewrite his career on a standard that didn't stabilize until 2019.
- **Demo:** 515 tags at the time; a Word file becomes a site live; content switches between themes, including the table-based site Bryan made in college.
- **How IDs heard it:** "the coolest thing I've seen all day"; being able to prototype a course live with a subject expert in the room; "stop copying from Word into the LMS"; template builders as paint-by-number versus "your painting on your wall."
- **Lines:** "I don't want humanity to need to code in order to put themselves on the internet." "The point of the academy is to push industry when it's stuck."

## HAX Ontology (Oct 2023, Bill Rose)

The course model summarized in `WORLDVIEW.md`: outcomes, pedagogy, structure, instruction, content and assessment, crossed by Understanding by Design at three points. "It doesn't need to be ideal. It does need to be valid."

## HAX Horizon Report (Oct 2023)

- Where HAX was: hax.psu.edu as a hosted service; every site is a `site.json` (JSON Outline Schema) plus static HTML pages, with about six backend calls in total. "When we blow up the notion of a database… we can build anything."
- Where it was going: see *Then and now* above. Penn State University Libraries' Beaumont and Fletcher plays site, built with HAX + 11ty in a containerized pipeline, was the model for scale.

## 2025: State of HAX Community (Feb 2025)

- HAX Lab founded; seven students working on the roadmap; College of Education faculty pursuing grants with HAX.
- DDD named by a student: "design, develop, destroy," once it's well designed it destroys the competition.
- The seven community pillars: accessible, efficient, extensible, free and open, platform agnostic, remixable, sustainable.
- "Meet our users where they are, not where we wish them to be." "You should not need to know what the web is to do everything with it."
- Goal for AI: "an ID and faculty BFF" for prototyping and growing rich online experiences.

## Nothing is intentional, until it is (Mar 2026)

- Opens with a hockey championship: ten years of work, three seconds of celebration. Users give you about three seconds.
- The outline designer as the start of Bryan's career and of his mission, ubiquity.
- HAX's UI from 2015 (talking to the screen, Kinect era) to 2026, and why every site since 2020 still works.

## The Sustainable Impact and Innovation Pipeline (SIIP): a Conversation with HAX

- **Recording:** [Apereo microconference, May 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNF0uzYi9Kk) (about 60 minutes)
- **Panel:** Bryan Ollendyke (HAX platform owner), Bill Rose (HAX product owner), Dr. Jennifer Frank and Dr. David Fusco (HAX Lab faculty), Winston White (HAX Lab intern), with former students Jimmy Kane, Ashley Santana and Alyssa Foran
- **Visuals:** [SIIP timeline](../visuals/README.md#siip-timeline-20082026)

### Structure
1. **What HAX is**, then a live demo of ubiquity: start an art portfolio from a skeleton, edit in place, take a photo straight into the page, swap themes over the same content, import a Pressbooks book by URL, import a Word lesson (videos become video players), download the site, edit it locally, export Markdown for AI, and publish to surge.
2. **The timeline.** HAX was a moonshot on top of ELMS:LN after about 19 years of online course delivery. Four full-time developers at the peak; developers left for industry; one remained. Turning the classroom into a contributor pipeline is what kept the rockets launching.
3. **What SIIP is.** Course → independent study, internship or club → contribution → the contribution comes back into the class. A spiral that widens each semester. "An endless machine of community growth and support."
4. **Stories.** Students and faculty answer Bryan's questions in their own words (see `WINS.md`).
5. **Why it works** (Bill Rose): cognitive apprenticeship. Traditional apprenticeship models, scaffolds, fades and coaches, and the work is visible. Schooling hides the thinking. Cognitive apprenticeship makes expert and student thinking visible, and real contributions to HAX give it an authentic context.
6. **Lessons and how to apply it elsewhere.** See SIIP lessons learned in `WINS.md`.
7. **Demo** (Dr. Fusco): Canvas course → AI readiness and AI-cheating reports → live HAX site, built on the student-made HAX CLI.

### Lines worth keeping
- "I want your voice on the internet without thinking about it, without learning anything. That is ubiquity." (Bryan)
- "Let's make web development feel like playing a video game." (Bryan's design challenge that started it, 2022)
- "I need empathetic design people that can think and code." (Bryan, on what Ashley taught him)
- "I don't have to sit there and convince anybody that this is an important project. They get it right away." (Dr. Frank, on students building for students)
- "HAX was one of the single most impactful things for my career." (Winston White)
- "If AI can install your project and learn from your docs, that likely means a junior developer can too." (Bryan)

## The Many Mes

- **Format:** workshop for instructional and learning design staff
- **Core idea:** build a digital "you" from context, skills, inputs, rules and outputs, and keep it outside any one product "so you don't lose you." PRAW is the worked example.
- **Evidence:** issue velocity by version ([visuals](../visuals/README.md#issue-velocity-by-hax-version)).

## So you want a (web) revolution?

- **Format:** conference talk introducing HAX
- **Core idea:** "My social cause: reduce the cost of web publishing to zero, regardless of ability." Telephone-to-smartphone phases as the arc from idea to ubiquity; HAX as hundreds of HTML tags, a course, a community and an ecosystem.
